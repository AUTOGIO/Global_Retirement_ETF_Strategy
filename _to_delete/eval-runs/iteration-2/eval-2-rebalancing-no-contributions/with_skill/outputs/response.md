# Rebalancing after an equity run

**Probably not — and not on a calendar in any case.** Your rebalancing runs through sales, so the question isn't "has it drifted?" but "has it drifted past a band wide enough to justify realising gains?" ## Why your case inverts the standard advice

The textbook answer — rebalance with new money, annually — assumes a contribution schedule. You have irregular top-ups and no withdrawals. That removes the free lever most of the time and leaves selling, which realises the USD gain **plus the embedded FX gain**. On a position held several years through BRL depreciation, the currency leg can be the larger half of the BRL-reported gain, and it's the half people forget when they eyeball "only a small trim."

The offsetting benefit is thinner than it looks. The rebalancing *risk control* is real; the rebalancing *premium* is not reliably there in equity/bond pairs, and the literature is genuinely mixed on it. So you're paying a certain tax cost for an uncertain return benefit and a modest risk benefit — which argues for wide bands, not tight ones.

And the risk benefit itself is weak for you right now. With no withdrawals, sequence-of-returns risk doesn't bind. An equity overweight in 2026 costs you drawdown depth you don't have to monetise.

## The policy I'd hold

| Item | Setting |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Threshold only. Never a date. |
| Equity sleeve band | ±10pp absolute around target (vs. the conventional ±5pp) |
| Within fixed income (nominal vs. inflation-linked) | ±10pp of the sleeve — intra-sleeve drift is close to costless |
| On breach | Correct to the **band edge**, not to target |
| Review cadence | Look twice a year and whenever a top-up lands. Looking is not trading. |

Free levers first, in order:

1. **Route the next top-up entirely to the most underweight sleeve.** On USD 200k, a USD 20k top-up closes a full 10pp gap without a single sale.
2. **If any holding distributes, stop reinvesting in place** — direct the cash to the underweight sleeve. Slow, but the tax event already happened; you may as well get the rebalancing for free.
3. **Sell only on a genuine breach**, and size it to the band edge.

On the FX leg both ways: an equity overweight is also a larger USD position, which is an active currency bet given you spend BRL. BRL depreciation flatters the sleeve in BRL terms; appreciation does the reverse. Neither is a reason to trade — just hold the position knowingly rather than by drift.

## What would change my answer

- **Withdrawals inside ~5 years.** Then sequence risk binds, bands tighten toward ±5pp, and the trim is worth its tax.
- **The stabiliser has become too small to sell into a crash.** Model a 35% equity drawdown. If the bond and inflation-linked sleeves left standing can't fund a plausible withdrawal need without selling equities into the hole, that's a real breach regardless of the tax bill.
- **A carry-forward loss position or an unusually low-basis lot mix** you can trim against — worth checking your IBKR lot report before assuming the cost.
- **Drift beyond ~15pp.** Past that the portfolio isn't the one you chose, and "wide bands" stops being a policy and becomes an excuse.
