# ETF main metrics

> **Authority note:** For live advice in this project, `src/retirement-etf-analyst/SKILL.md` and `references/brazil-tax-ibkr.md` supersede this document on tax. Do **not** assert Brazilian (or other) tax rates, exemption limits, or filing thresholds here or in answers — name mechanisms and route specifics to an accountant. Numeric rate examples below were removed for that reason.

Here is a unified **ETF Due Diligence Framework** that merges structural completeness with execution-oriented pragmatism.  
To make it immediately actionable, the metrics are organized into **four priority tiers**—starting with non-negotiable structural and tax dealbreakers, moving through the true costs of ownership and portfolio reality checks, and demoting backward-looking historical returns to the bottom as sanity checks.  
**The Complete ETF Due Diligence Matrix**  

| Metric | Category | What It Measures | Why It Matters / Pragmatic Interpretation | Key Target & Actionable Guidance |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Domicile | Tier 1: Dealbreakers | The legal jurisdiction where the fund is registered (e.g., U.S., Ireland). | The #1 highest-leverage field for non-U.S. investors. Drives fund-level and investor-level withholding outcomes, and can change U.S. estate-tax situs exposure on U.S.-domiciled holdings. Exact rates and thresholds change and are fact-specific — do not quote them from memory. | Prefer comparing U.S.-domiciled vs Irish UCITS equivalents on structure and after-tax *mechanics*; confirm numbers with current issuer docs and an accountant. |
| 2. Benchmark & Cap Scope | Tier 1: Dealbreakers | The exact index replicated (e.g., MSCI ACWI vs. FTSE Global All Cap vs. MSCI ACWI IMI). | Determines what you actually own. A holdings count of 2,238 (ACWI) vs. 10,000+ (VT) isn't "worse"—it reflects a different scope (Large/Mid cap only vs. All-Cap). | Ensure index rules match your desired reach; do not rely on fund names like "Global" or "World." |
| 3. Issuer & Inception | Tier 1: Dealbreakers | Managing institution (Vanguard, BlackRock, State Street) and launch date. | Drives operational trust, securities-lending safety, and product longevity. | Top-tier issuers have near-zero closure risk; apply strict age/size filters only to niche or thematic ETFs. |
| 4. Tracking Difference | Tier 2: Real Costs | Realized fund return minus benchmark return ($R_{\\text{ETF}} - R_{\\text{Index}}$). | The honest total cost of ownership.Captures stated fees net of securities-lending revenue and internal cash drag. A fund can have a higher expense ratio but a better tracking difference. | Look for tracking difference as close to 0.00%(or slightly positive) as possible over 3–5 year periods. |
| 5. Stated Expense Ratio | Tier 2: Real Costs | Annual percentage fee deducted directly from fund assets. | A direct, guaranteed compounding drag on long-term returns, but not the final word on efficiency (see Tracking Difference). | Target <0.10%for broad market index ETFs. |
| 6. The Execution Stack (Spread + Premium/Discount) | Tier 2: Real Costs | Live bid-ask spread combined with whether the ETF trades above/below its Net Asset Value (NAV). | Your immediate upfront entry/exit tax.A 0.15% NAV premium plus a 0.05% spread stacks as an immediate 0.20% round-trip surcharge on your broker (e.g., IBKR). | Check live spreads and premiums during market hours before executing—especially critical for smaller funds like SPGM. |
| 7. Portfolio Turnover Ratio | Tier 2: Real Costs | The percentage of the fund's basket replaced over a 12-month period. | Reveals internal trading friction, brokerage commissions, and capital gains tax drag. | Passive broad-market index ETFs should maintain turnover <10%(typically <5%). |
| 8. Country & Regional Weight | Tier 3: Portfolio Reality | Percentage distribution across geographic regions. | Shatters the "Global" illusion. Market-cap-weighted global ETFs are 60%+ U.S. equityby definition. Buying a "World" fund does not diversify you away from U.S. macro risk. | Check regional weights to ensure your overall portfolio isn't unintentionally over-indexed to a single country. |
| 9. Top-10 Concentration | Tier 3: Portfolio Reality | Combined weight of the top 10 individual stock holdings. | Highlights single-stock mega-cap dependency. Cap-weighted global funds are currently heavily concentrated in U.S. tech giants (Apple, NVIDIA, Microsoft). | Verify overlap against any individual stock picks or US-specific tech ETFs you hold elsewhere. |
| 10. Valuation Multiples ($P/E$, $P/B$ Ratios) | Tier 3: Portfolio Reality | Weighted average Price-to-Earnings ($P/E$) and Price-to-Book ($P/B$) ratios. | Forward-looking valuation snapshot.Tells you whether the underlying equity basket is historically expensive or discounted relative to corporate earnings. | Use to assess fundamental valuation risk and expected long-term return potential across different asset classes. |
| 11. Yield & Distribution Schedule | Tier 4: Income Mechanics | Annualized yield and payout frequency (Semi-Annual, Quarterly, etc.). | Dictates cash-flow timing and how often dividend withholding tax hits your account(e.g., ACWI pays semi-annually). | Match distribution frequency to your cash flow needs; note whether income is distributed or accumulated. |
| 12. Assets Under Management (AUM) | Tier 4: Income Mechanics | Total capital held in the fund. | A proxy for secondary market liquidity and fund viability. While SPGM (~$1.6B) is smaller than VT (~$97B), both are well above danger thresholds. | Prefer core holdings with >$500M–$1B+in AUM to ensure tight market-maker spreads. |
| 13. Trailing Returns (1Y / 3Y / 5Y) | Tier 5: Sanity Checks | Historical trailing performance over various timeframes. | Backward-looking noise. The weakest metric for choosing between index funds in the same asset class. Minor differences reflect index scope, not fund superiority. | Use solely as a sanity check to confirm the ETF is tracking its stated asset class correctly. |
| 14. Volatility & Drawdown(Standard Deviation / Max Drawdown) | Tier 5: Sanity Checks | Dispersion of historical returns and worst peak-to-trough drop. | Measures historical asset class risk. Within the exact same equity category (e.g., VT vs. ACWI vs. SPGM), these numbers will be nearly identical. | Use to size risk across different asset classes (e.g., equities vs. bonds), not to differentiate similar index ETFs. |
  
**How to Execute This Framework in Practice**  
1. **Start at Tier 1 (Dealbreakers):** Immediately disqualify funds with unfavorable **Domicile** tax rules or an underlying **Benchmark Scope** that does not fit your portfolio strategy.  
2. **Evaluate Tier 2 (Real Costs):** Skip the Expense Ratio comparison and look directly at **Tracking Difference** to see who runs the fund cheapest in reality. Before placing a trade, check the live **Execution Stack** (Spread + NAV Premium/Discount) on your brokerage platform.  
3. **Check Tier 3 (Portfolio Reality):** Confirm that the **Country Weighting**, **Top-10 Concentration**, and **Valuation Multiples** align with your actual diversification goals rather than relying on labels like "All-World."  
4. **Ignore Tier 5 for Fund Selection:** Never select an ETF over an equivalent peer simply because its trailing 3-year return was **0.2%** higher; treat historical performance strictly as a functional replication test.
